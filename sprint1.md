Overview (~0.33 pgs): 

Identify the technical area your group has chosen and explain why you selected it. Describe how the topic is relevant to computer science and how it extends your knowledge beyond what you already know.

If your project builds on a previous course, clearly state where that course ended and how you plan to go further. Briefly summarize any prior experience group members bring to this topic.

Conclude with a short explanation of how your project fulfills the social impact requirement: How does this work make the world better for a community, user group, or broader audience?
Goals (~0.25 pgs):

List the specific knowledge, skills, and concepts you plan to learn in order to complete your project. State what you will understand or be able to do by the end of the semester that you could not do at the beginning.

(You may find it helpful to draft this section alongside Section 3 as you refine the scope of your topic.)
Learning Resources and Strategies (~1 pg) 

Explain the methods and resources your group will use to learn the required technical content AND to understand your chosen social impact area.

Include:

    Specific resources (e.g., LinkedIn Learning courses, online tutorials, documentation, books, research papers, datasets).
    How you will use each resource (e.g., complete a course module, follow a guided tutorial, read a textbook chapter, prototype using a tool).
    Learning strategies (e.g., pair programming, weekly study sessions, dividing subtopics, experimentation, consultations with experts).

Your resource list should be concrete and show that you have researched what is available to support your learning.
Timeline (~0.5 pgs):

Provide a rough week-by-week timeline showing when your group expects to complete major learning goals and milestones.

Include:

    Dates for finishing key learning resources (from Section 3)
    Target dates for technical prototypes or checkpoints
    Alignment with Sprints 2–4, expanding on those with more detailed milestones

The timeline should demonstrate a realistic plan for sustained progress throughout the semester.
Learning Demonstration (~0.5 pgs):

Describe the concrete evidence and artifacts you will produce to show that you achieved your learning goals. Be specific about the form this evidence will take—for example:

    “An AR game implemented in Unity which teaches middle school students about common cybersecurity threats,” rather than “a Unity game”
    “A web app using Django which could help food pantries coordinate volunteer shifts more efficiently” rather than “a web app using modern frameworks”

Evidence may include code, interactive demos or tutorials, videos, prototypes, visualizations, datasets, or other technical artifacts.

Don’t forget written components such as documentation, tutorials, or technical write-ups. Note that the evidence may evolve as your project develops, but provide the most complete vision you can at this stage.
